# Voice-of-customer method

## What each source over-represents
| Source | Over-represents | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| App-store and marketplace reviews | the delighted and the furious; recent updates | breakage, pricing anger, first-run problems |
| Forums and community spaces | power users, people with time | workflows, workarounds, depth |
| Issue trackers | technical users | precise failure descriptions, how long things stay unfixed |
| Social posts | the loud and the promotional | moments of delight and outrage; weak for depth |
| Switching and "alternative to" threads | people who left or are leaving | push, pull, anxiety, habit |
| Vendor testimonials and case studies | the vendor's ideal customer | the positioning the vendor wants; not user voice |
| Support and help-centre articles | common points of confusion | where users get stuck |

Nobody writes a review to say "it was fine". The silent majority is missing from every source; say so.

## Coding labels
| Label | The user is saying | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pain | "this blocks or slows me" | size of the problem |
| delight | "this is why I stay" | what must be preserved |
| workaround | "so I built or do this instead" | strongest evidence of an unmet need |
| request | "I wish it had …" | a clue; find the need behind it |
| switch-from | "I left because …" | push |
| switch-to | "I chose it because …" | pull |
| confusion | "I don't understand how …" | usability and positioning problems |

## From request to need
Ask of every request: what were they trying to do when they wanted this? "Add folders" may mean "I cannot find things", which search, tags or better recall could also solve. Record the need; list the requested solution as one option.

## Four forces of progress
```
            push of the situation  ─┐                ┌─ anxiety about the new
                                    ├─> switch <─────┤
            pull of the new         ─┘                └─ habit of the present
```
- Push: frustration with the current way. Look for "I was tired of", "it kept", "every time".
- Pull: the attraction of the new. Look for "I saw that it could", "finally".
- Anxiety: fear about the new. Look for "I was worried", "what if", "will my data", "lock-in".
- Habit: inertia. Look for "I've used X for years", "all my stuff is in".

Design implications: raise pull with a clear first win; lower anxiety with import, export, trial and visible data control; lower habit cost with compatibility and migration.

## Kano categories
| Category | When present | When absent | How to spot it in user voice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Must-be | unnoticed | anger | mentioned only in complaints ("it lost my data", "no export") |
| Performance | satisfaction grows with more | dissatisfaction | users compare products on it (speed, accuracy, limits, price) |
| Attractive | delight | nothing | surprise in praise ("I didn't expect", "magic") |
| Indifferent | nothing | nothing | on the vendor's feature list, absent from user voice |
| Reverse | annoyance | relief | "I wish I could turn off …" |

Categories decay: attractive becomes performance, then must-be. Date the evidence.

Implication: meet every must-be before anything else; choose one or two performance dimensions to win; add a small number of attractive touches; drop the indifferent.

## Ranking themes
Note four things for each theme and keep them apart:
- **severity**: blocks the job, slows it, or annoys;
- **frequency**: people reporting it, and from how many independent sources;
- **recency**: still reported after the latest release, or only in old posts;
- **leaving**: whether anyone says they left, downgraded or stopped paying over it.

Order by severity, then by people, and say why. Do not multiply the four into one score: a product hides which of them is weak, and a severe pain nobody left over is a different finding from a mild one that drove people away.

## Honest wording
| Evidence | Say |
| --- | --- |
| 1–2 quotes | "one reviewer", "an isolated report" |
| 3–5 quotes, 2+ sources | "several users report" |
| many quotes across source kinds | "a recurring theme across reviews and forums" |
| any self-selected sample | never a percentage of "users" |

## Privacy and ethics
- Quote public posts; cite the page. Do not copy usernames, profile details or anything that identifies a private person into reports.
- Do not use quotes from private groups, leaked material or content behind a login the user has not provided.
