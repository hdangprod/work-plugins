# Market mapping method

## Four kinds of alternative
| Kind | Definition | Example for "remember what I read" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | same job, same approach | another read-later app with highlights |
| Indirect | same job, different approach | an AI assistant that searches your browser history |
| Substitute | a general tool bent to the job | bookmarks, a notes file, emailing links to yourself |
| Non-consumption | doing nothing or relying on memory | re-searching the web each time |

The substitute and non-consumption rows are usually the largest part of the market and the hardest competitors: they are free, familiar and already installed.

## Segments
A segment is a group that shares a situation and a need, and would respond to the same offer. Useful ways to cut:
- frequency and stakes of the job (daily habit or rare emergency);
- solo or shared;
- tolerance for setup and maintenance;
- sensitivity about data (private, regulated, public);
- willingness to pay and who pays;
- technical skill.

For each segment record: job, current solution, willingness to pay, deal-breakers, where they talk to each other.

## Choosing axes
A good axis:
- is something buyers compare on when choosing (evidence: switching stories, "vs" posts);
- spreads the products out;
- represents a real trade-off, so that both ends are legitimate choices;
- matters to the segment we care about.

Test the axes by asking whether moving along one would cost a product something. If not, it is a quality score, not a positioning axis.

Try two or three pairs of axes before settling. A different pair often shows a different gap.

## White space
| Reading | Signs | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nobody wants it | no workarounds, no forum requests, earlier attempts shut down | search for failed products and their post-mortems |
| Cannot be built or paid for | technically hard, costs exceed willingness to pay, a platform forbids it | check costs, terms of service, platform rules |
| Real opening | users build workarounds, requests are old and frequent, incumbents are structurally unable to serve it | count independent requests; explain why incumbents will not move |

A real opening needs a reason why incumbents leave it empty. "They have not thought of it" is almost never the reason.

## Sizing without fooling yourself
- Prefer bottom-up: number of people with the job × share who would pay × price. State every factor's source and year.
- Top-down numbers from market reports are context at best. Quote them only with publisher, year and definition, and mark them tentative.
- For a personal or small project, size is often the wrong question. Ask instead: are there enough people like the owner to matter, and is anyone serving them well?
- Traction signals, weakest to strongest: press mentions, funding, downloads, ratings count, active community, published paying-customer numbers, filings.

## Dynamics
- What changed in the last year (a platform capability, a price drop, a regulation) that makes a new position possible now?
- Who is being bundled into a larger product, and what happens to standalone products when that occurs?
- Which products are converging on the same position?

"Why now?" needs an answer. If nothing changed, ask why the opening has not been taken already.

## Output discipline
- A table of alternatives with the same columns for each, cells cited.
- One map (two axes) with a sentence per placement. A text table is enough; a picture is optional.
- Three to five segments.
- White space with its three-way reading.
- A shortlist with reasons.
