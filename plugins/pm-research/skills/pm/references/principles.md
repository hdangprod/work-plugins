# The ten principles in practice

Each principle with the behaviour it asks for and the failure it prevents.

## 1. Decision first
- Do: open every run with `Decision:` and with the default action if nothing is learned.
- Test: for each research question ask "which answer would move us off the default?" Drop questions with no such answer.
- Failure it prevents: a long, accurate report nobody can act on.

## 2. Problem before solution
- Do: describe the job the user is trying to get done, how they do it today, and what that costs in time, money, risk or annoyance.
- Test: can the problem be stated without naming any product or feature?
- Failure: comparing feature lists for a problem nobody has.

## 3. Outcomes, not output
- Do: express value as a change in user behaviour or result ("finds a note in under 10 seconds"), then ask which features cause it.
- Failure: "competitor X has 40 integrations, we need 40".

## 4. Source and confidence for every claim
- Do: record the verbatim quote first, then the claim. Label each claim `fact`, `inference` or `unknown`.
- Confidence: **C** confirmed (a first-hand source says it and verification agreed), **T** tentative (verified, but only third-party or community sources carry it), **U** not established (unchecked, partial, refuted, contested or unreachable).
- Failure: the fluent paragraph with one invented number in the middle.

## 5. Competition is wider than competitors
- Do: list four kinds for every job: direct (same job, same way), indirect (same job, different way), substitute (a general tool bent to the job: spreadsheet, notes app, chat), and non-consumption (doing nothing, doing it by hand).
- Failure: beating three named rivals and losing to a spreadsheet.

## 6. Riskiest assumption
- Do: for each option, write the one belief which, if false, makes the option worthless, and the cheapest test that could falsify it within days.
- Cagan's four risks give the candidates: **value** (will they choose it), **usability** (can they work it out), **feasibility** (can we build it), **viability** (does it work for the business: cost, legal, support, channel).
- Failure: six months of building before learning that nobody wanted it.

## 7. Several options
- Do: at least three options that differ in kind, not three sizes of the same idea. Always include doing nothing or waiting.
- Failure: a recommendation that was the only idea considered.

## 8. Non-goals
- Do: list what is out, and why, next to what is in.
- Failure: scope that grows because nothing was ever excluded.

## 9. Confidence and what would change it
- Do: `Confidence: NN%` plus the observation that would reverse the recommendation. Then record a dated prediction with `pmr predict add` so the confidence can be scored later.
- Failure: certainty that cannot be checked, and no way to learn whether 80% meant 80%.

## 10. Show contradictions
- Do: when two sources disagree, keep both under a `## Contradictions` heading, say which you weight more and why, and what would settle it.
- Failure: a tidy story that hid the one fact that mattered.

## Habits of good product people
- They ask "for whom?" and "compared with what?" before "how?".
- They go to first-hand material: the product itself, the pricing page, the changelog, the user's own words.
- They separate what a company says (positioning), what the product does (behaviour) and what users experience (reviews). The gaps between the three are where opportunities live.
- They read a changelog as a strategy document: what a team ships and stops shipping shows where it is going.
- They size a decision by reversibility: fast and rough for two-way doors, slow and careful for one-way doors.
- They write the recommendation so a busy reader gets it from the first five lines.
- They think with frameworks and print only what the thinking found. A grid nobody asked for is a cost to the reader, not rigour.
- They hold a view and say it, including when the owner's own belief has nothing under it.
- They look for the cheap fix (a setting, a habit, a small script) before the project.
- They keep score of their own predictions.
