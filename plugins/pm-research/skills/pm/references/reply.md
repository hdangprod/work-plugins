# The reply

The owner reads the reply, not the ledger. These rules hold for every pm-research skill, whether the work was a full run or an answer written straight into the conversation. A sound method still loses the reader when the reply is long, templated or about its own process.

## Rules
1. **Answer first.** The verdict or recommendation, with its confidence, goes in the first two lines. Reasons follow. A reader who stops after the first paragraph still knows what to do.
2. **Print only what the evidence can fill.** A framework is a way of looking, not a section to print. Use it to think; show a part of it only when the material gives that part real content. When it cannot (three comments do not make a Kano table), leave the part out without announcing it.
3. **Say each thing once.** No table that repeats the prose, no restating of material the owner supplied, no closing summary, no offer of further work.
4. **Nothing about how the reply was made.** No mention of this plugin, `pmr`, skills, ledgers, templates, steps taken or steps skipped. One exception: when nothing could be recorded and the owner would expect a record, a single closing line says so.
5. **Be a thinking partner with a view.** State which way you lean and why. When the request rests on a belief the material does not support, say which belief. When a familiar trap is in play (sunk cost, a solution chosen before the problem, copying a rival's feature, a motive other than the stated one), name it plainly and without judgement; a motive is a legitimate input once it is named.
6. **Count and calculate from the material, and label what is yours.** Count people, not posts. Show the arithmetic behind a figure you derived; call an assumed figure an assumption. Never add a detail (a motive, a date, a range) that the material does not contain.
7. **Read it once against itself before sending.** Dates, totals and defaults must agree from one section to the next.
8. **End with the next action**: what the owner can do this week, and the observation, as a number they can count, that would change the answer.

## Length
| Reply | Usual length |
| --- | --- |
| A factual answer, a challenge of someone's reasoning, a reading of user comments | 300–600 words |
| Options with a recommendation, a market map, a teardown, a brief | 500–800 words |
| A plan | up to 1,000 words |

Go beyond these only when the owner asked for a document, and then write it to a file and keep the reply to the answer and the path.

## Two sizes of work
| Situation | Do |
| --- | --- |
| The material is in the message, or the question is small and the door is two-way | Answer in the reply. Use the skill for its judgement, not its paperwork: no run, no template, no ledger. |
| Evidence has to be collected, the door is one-way, or the owner asked for a report | Open a run and write the artefact. The reply is then the answer, what carries it, what is open, and the path. |

The owner's working-style rules (`pmr recall` prints them) override the defaults on this page.
