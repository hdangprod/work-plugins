# Teardown method

## Jobs to be done
People do not buy products; they hire them to make progress in a situation. The same product is hired for different jobs by different people, and its real competitors are whatever else could do that job.

Job statement: **When** <situation>, **I want to** <motivation>, **so that** <outcome>.

- Functional job: the task ("find what I wrote about a topic last year").
- Emotional job: how they want to feel ("not anxious that I lost it").
- Social job: how they want to be seen ("organised, on top of things").

Find jobs in user voice: reviews that begin "I use it to …", switching stories ("I moved from X because …"), and questions in forums ("how do I … with <product>?").

## Positioning (April Dunford's five components, in this order)
1. **Competitive alternatives**: what the customer would do if the product did not exist.
2. **Unique attributes**: what it has that those alternatives lack.
3. **Value**: what those attributes enable for the customer, with proof.
4. **Target segment**: who cares most about that value.
5. **Market category**: the frame it uses so the value is obvious ("AI notebook", "second brain", "team wiki").

Reading a competitor's positioning backwards tells you who they have decided not to serve.

## Question bank
**Company and direction**
- Who is behind it, how is it funded, how does it make money?
- What did it ship in the last 12 months? What did it stop mentioning?
- What is it hiring for?

**Product**
- What is the core loop, and how long from sign-up to first value?
- What must the user bring or set up (data, integrations, habits)?
- What are the hard limits (quotas, context, file sizes, platforms, regions)?
- What does it deliberately not do?
- How does it handle the user's data: storage, export, deletion, training use?
- How open is it: API, export formats, plugins, standards?

**Money**
- What is the unit of charge (seat, usage, storage, feature gate)?
- What does the free tier withhold, and what triggers the upgrade?
- What does it cost a typical user per month in practice?

**Users**
- Who praises it and for what, in their words?
- What are the top three complaints, and how old are they?
- What workarounds do users build? (Each one is an unmet need.)
- Why do people leave, and where do they go?

**Moat**
- What would a well-funded copy still lack after a year?
- Does it get better with more users or more of the user's own data?
- How painful is it to switch away?

## Where to look
| For | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| Positioning | home page headline and sub-headline, pricing page plan names, comparison pages the vendor publishes |
| Real behaviour | docs, API reference, help centre, changelog, demo and tutorial videos, public templates |
| Limits | quota and rate-limit docs, terms of service, footnotes on the pricing page |
| Direction | release notes over time, roadmap page, job posts, conference talks, founder interviews |
| Pain | low-rated store reviews, forum threads sorted by replies, issue trackers sorted by reactions, "alternative to" and "vs" searches |
| Switching | migration guides (who writes them and in which direction), import and export tools |
| Data handling | privacy policy, data-processing terms, security page |

## Kinds of weakness
| Kind | Sign | Meaning for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural | follows from the business model, architecture or main customer; complaints are years old | durable opening |
| Neglected | fixable, but the team's attention is elsewhere | opening for now |
| In progress | recent changelog entries address it | not an opening |
| By design | a trade-off the target segment accepts | opening only for a different segment |

## Kinds of strength
| Kind | Example | Can we copy it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feature | a clever capture shortcut | yes, quickly |
| Experience | the whole flow feels effortless | yes, with taste and time |
| Integration | deep hooks into tools users already live in | slowly |
| Data or network | improves with accumulated data or more users | hardly |
| Distribution | bundled with something everyone already has | no |
| Cost | can price lower because of scale or ownership | no |

Copy features, learn from experiences, respect the rest and route around it.

## From teardown to action
For every item in **copy / avoid / exploit**, state: the user need behind it, the evidence, and how confident you are. "Exploit" items must rest on structural or by-design weaknesses; anything else is temporary.
